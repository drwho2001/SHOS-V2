SHOS — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 September 2026

In one sentence: SHOS has no backend and no user accounts — every record you create is stored, encrypted, only on your own device, and nothing about your health, contacts, or activity is ever sent to us, because there is no "us" your data is sent to.

Who this policy covers

SHOS ("the app") is a personal sexual health and lifestyle tracker built by a single independent developer. This policy applies to the SHOS Android app and its web/PWA build. There is no company, server infrastructure, or account system operated by the developer that processes your data — this policy is short because there is genuinely very little for it to describe.

What data the app collects

All data you enter into SHOS — contacts, encounters, test results, medications, symptoms, clinic visits, and every other record type — is created and stored entirely on your own device, in your device's local app storage. It is encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) the moment it's written. The developer never receives, sees, or has access to any of it, at any point, because it never leaves your device unless you personally choose to export it (see "Your own exports" below).

The app does not:

  • require you to create an account or sign in
  • collect your name, email address, or any identifying information for itself
  • use analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs of any kind
  • sync data to any cloud service
  • share, sell, or transmit your data to any third party

Your own exports

SHOS lets you export a backup of your own data (as a plain or password-encrypted file) to your device's own storage or share sheet, entirely at your own initiative. Where that file goes afterward — a cloud drive, an email to yourself, a USB drive — is entirely under your control; the app has no part in that beyond the export itself, and no copy is retained anywhere else.

Optional features that use the network

SHOS is designed to work fully offline. Two optional features make outbound network requests, both off by default configurability and both visible/toggleable in Settings > Security & Privacy > Data & network:

  • Address lookup. If you use the optional "look up an address" helper when adding a Location, the text you type is sent to OpenStreetMap's public Nominatim search API to return matching addresses. If you instead use the optional "use current location" button, your device's real GPS coordinates are sent to that same Nominatim service to resolve them into a place name/address — a more sensitive request than a typed search, since it's your actual location, so it gets the same explicit toggle and only ever happens when you actively tap that button, never automatically or in the background.
  • Update check. The app periodically checks GitHub's public API to see whether a newer version is available. This is a generic, anonymous request for release metadata — it does not include any of your personal data.

Both can be turned off from that same Settings screen; the app functions fully without them.

Device permissions

The Android app requests the following permissions, each used only for the specific feature named — never collected or transmitted anywhere:

  • Notifications — for medication, testing, and clinic-visit reminders you configure yourself.
  • Location (coarse/fine) — only if you tap "use current location" when adding a Location; never accessed in the background.
  • Calendar (read/write) — only if you turn on calendar sync, and only ever writes to a private, local-only calendar the app creates on your device; never reads or touches your existing calendars.
  • Storage (legacy, capped to older Android versions) — used to save a backup file you export yourself.
  • Biometric — used only as a local unlock gate for the app's own PIN/App Lock feature (via the Android system biometric prompt). The app never receives or stores your fingerprint/face data itself — that stays entirely within Android's own secure biometric system.

Data deletion

Since nothing is stored anywhere except your own device, deleting your data is entirely in your hands: remove individual records in-app, clear all app data from Android's own App Info screen, or uninstall the app. There is no server-side copy to separately request deletion of, because none exists.

Children's privacy

SHOS is intended for adult users (UK sexual-health context) and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect data from children, and as a matter of the app's own design, it does not collect data from anyone at all — see above.
